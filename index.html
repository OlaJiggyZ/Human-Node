        Liability

        You answer for what your AI says.

        In February 2024 a Canadian tribunal held Air Canada responsible for its website chatbot's wrong bereavement-fare answer.

        Case
        Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024 BCCRT 149
        Error
        The chatbot said a reduced bereavement fare could be claimed after travel. The airline's policy did not allow it.
        Finding
        The chatbot is part of the airline's website. Air Canada was liable for negligent misrepresentation.
        Outcome
        C$812.02 in damages, interest and fees. The reasoning is widely cited.

        The problem

        Cyber risk is a decision problem.

        People are involved in around 60% of breaches (Verizon DBIR 2025). In 2026 a cloned CEO voice moved €95M at Fideuram (Reuters), and a trusted internal service let about $6.3B leave Binance in roughly 20 minutes (TechCrunch). People, signing systems and AI agents now sit on one decision surface.

        Illuminate

        Reveal

        The points where decisions are made under pressure: help desks, approvals, signing systems, supplier channels and AI agents.

        Model

        Measure

        Internal and external forces resolved into a measurable risk layer that locates likely decision failure.

        Reinforce

        Strengthen

        Model-driven controls at the decision points most open to manipulation.

        BTO model and DHOS

        Two vantage points. One decision-risk map.

        The BTO model fuses internal and external intelligence into a single map of where failure is most likely to be induced.

        DHOS is the assessment model underneath. Structured assessments, scenario exercises and threat intelligence set a decision baseline for key roles, track change between assessments and locate where failure is most likely.
